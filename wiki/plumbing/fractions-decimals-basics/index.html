Fractions & Decimals in Plumbing Calculations

Written by John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-13
Fact Checked

Plumbing is a trade of precision. Whether you are calculating the center-to-center measurements of a pipe run or translating fitting dimensions, you must be able to manipulate fractions and decimals quickly and accurately on the job site without a calculator.

1. Reading the Ruler: Piping Fractions

Standard tape measures used in the United States are divided into inches, with each inch further subdivided into halves (1/2"), quarters (1/4"), eighths (1/8"), sixteenths (1/16"), and sometimes thirty-seconds (1/32"). As a journeyman, you must immediately recognize these increments to avoid layout errors.

  • 1/8" = 0.125 inches
  • 1/4" = 0.25 inches
  • 3/8" = 0.375 inches
  • 1/2" = 0.5 inches
  • 5/8" = 0.625 inches
  • 3/4" = 0.75 inches
  • 7/8" = 0.875 inches

2. Calculating Fitting Allowance (Take-Off)

To find the actual cut length of a pipe, you must calculate the "take-off" or fitting allowance. The formula is:

Cut Length = Center-to-Center Distance - (Fitting Take-Off 1 + Fitting Take-Off 2)

For example, if you have a center-to-center measurement of 14 3/4" and you are using two 90-degree elbows that each have a take-off of 7/8", your math will look like:

Total Take-Off = 7/8" + 7/8" = 14/8" = 1 6/8" = 1 3/4"
Cut Length = 14 3/4" - 1 3/4" = 13 inches.

3. Converting Fractions to Decimals

Many complex trade calculations (such as calculating offsets or sizing pump flows) require decimal entries. To convert any fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.

Fraction to Decimal = Numerator / Denominator

If a pipe diameter is 3/4", the decimal equivalent is: 3 / 4 = 0.75". Knowing these common conversions by heart increases your speed and accuracy during your licensing exam.
