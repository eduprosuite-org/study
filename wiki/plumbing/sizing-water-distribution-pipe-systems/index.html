Sizing Water Distribution Piping

Written by John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-13
Fact Checked

Sizing water distribution systems ensures that fixtures receive adequate flow rate and pressure during peak usage. Sizing calculations rely on converting fixture counts to fixture units and factoring in pipe friction losses.

1. Water Supply Fixture Units (WSFU)

Similar to drainage design (DFUs), water systems are sized using **Water Supply Fixture Units (WSFUs)**. WSFUs represent the load value of a fixture under peak conditions:

  • Private Water Closet (Flush Tank): 2.2 WSFU
  • Public Water Closet (Flushometer): 10.0 WSFU
  • Private Lavatory Sink: 0.7 WSFU
  • Kitchen Sink: 1.4 WSFU

2. Using Hunter's Curve

You cannot simply add WSFU values together and size pipes based on a linear scale. Plumbers convert cumulative WSFU counts into **Gallons Per Minute (GPM)** flow demand using **Hunter's Curve** (IPC Appendix E). This curve accounts for the statistical probability that not all fixtures will be flushed simultaneously.

3. Factoring Friction Loss

As water flows through pipes, friction causes pressure drop. You must calculate pressure drops using the **Hazen-Williams formula** or standard sizing charts. Max velocity must be limited to **8 feet per second** for copper lines and **10 feet per second** for plastic lines to prevent noise and erosion.
