What is a Double Check Valve?

Curator: John Masterson | Master Plumber
Fact Checked: Active
Updated: 2026-08-16
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A double check valve assembly (DCVA) is a backflow preventer consisting of two inline, spring-loaded check valves, shutoff valves, and test cocks. Because it lacks an atmospheric relief port, it is strictly approved only for low-hazard (non-toxic) applications.

Core Concept & Mechanism

The double check valve assembly (DCVA) is designed to protect potable water systems from both backpressure and backsiphonage. The system consists of two independently acting, spring-loaded check valves in series. Under normal forward flow, the water pressure overrides the internal spring tension (usually calibrated to require about 1 to 2 PSI to open), allowing water to pass.

If a backflow condition arises, the springs snap the check disks closed. Having two checks in series provides redundancy: if one check valve is jammed open by scale or debris, the second check valve still holds the seal. However, because a DCVA has a closed outer body with no differential relief valve or atmospheric drain port (unlike an RPZ), a double failure of both check valves will allow contaminated water to cross-connect with the clean water supply. For this reason, plumbing codes strictly forbid using DCVAs on high-hazard systems like chemical processing lines or boiler makeup water containing corrosion inhibitors.

Code & Technical Regulations

Both major model codes specify the exact applications, testing requirements, and clearances for ASSE 1015 double check assemblies:

Regulatory Parameter IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Approved Standard Section 608.13.7: Must comply with ASSE 1015 or AWWA C510. Section 603.5.6: Must comply with ASSE 1015.
Hazard Limits Table 608.1: Rated for Low-Hazard backpressure and backsiphonage only. Table 603.2: Only allowed for non-health hazard (low-hazard) connections.
Testing Protocol Section 608.14: Must be field tested on installation and annually by a certified tester. Section 603.2: Annual field testing is mandatory using the four integral test cocks.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

  • ASSE Mappings: On both the Journeyman Plumber Exam and the Master Contractor Exam, you must link the mechanical backflow preventers with their ASSE standards. Double check valves are **ASSE 1015**.
  • Hazard Level Identification: If a test question asks which device can protect a domestic line from a lawn sprinkler containing liquid fertilizer, a DCVA is **incorrect** because fertilizer is toxic (high-hazard). You must select an RPZ (ASSE 1013) or PVB (ASSE 1020).
  • DCVA vs. RPZ: Remember that a DCVA is a sealed system with no relief port. It can be installed vertically or horizontally (depending on listing), whereas an RPZ must typically be installed horizontally and has a visible discharge port.
