What is a Backflow Preventer?

Curator: John Masterson | Master Plumber
Fact Checked: Active
Updated: 2026-08-16
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A backflow preventer is a mechanical assembly or device designed to be installed on water supply lines to permit liquid flow in only one direction. It prevents contaminated water from entering the municipal potable system due to backpressure or backsiphonage.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Unlike a simple check valve, a certified backflow prevention assembly includes specific test cocks and shutoff valves that allow for field testing to ensure the internal components are operating correctly. The choice of preventer hinges on the hazard classification:

  • Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ / RP): Consists of two independently acting spring-loaded check valves with a hydraulically operating differential relief valve located between them. If either check valve leaks, the relief valve opens to discharge the water to the atmosphere, guaranteeing safety even in high-hazard backpressure scenarios.
  • Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA / DC): Features two check valves but lacks an atmospheric relief valve. Consequently, it is restricted to low-hazard environments where backflow would only cause aesthetic issues rather than health hazards.
  • Vacuum Breakers (PVB, SVB, AVB): Use a floating check disk to seal an air inlet during normal flow. If a siphonage condition occurs, the disk drops, venting the system to the atmosphere and breaking the vacuum.

Code & Technical Regulations

The type of backflow prevention assembly and its testing schedule are strictly defined in both IPC Section 608 and UPC Section 603:

Device Type Standard / Rating IPC Sizing & Rules UPC Sizing & Rules
RPZ Assembly ASSE 1013 High & Low Hazard; Backpressure/Backsiphonage High & Low Hazard; Backpressure/Backsiphonage
Double Check Assembly ASSE 1015 Low Hazard only; Backpressure/Backsiphonage Low Hazard only; Backpressure/Backsiphonage
Pressure Vacuum Breaker ASSE 1020 High & Low Hazard; Backsiphonage only High & Low Hazard; Backsiphonage only
Annual Testing Required Section 608.14 (Test upon installation and annually) Section 603.2 (Test upon installation and annually)

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

  • Testing Requirements: Both the Journeyman Plumber Exam and the Master Contractor Exam require you to know that mechanical backflow preventer assemblies must be tested **at the time of installation, immediately after repairs, and annually thereafter**.
  • Device Limitations: Memorize that AVBs (ASSE 1001) cannot be subjected to continuous pressure (meaning pressurized for more than 12 hours out of any 24-hour period) and cannot have valves downstream of the device.
  • RPZ Relief Vent: Master Plumber candidates should know that an RPZ relief port must vent to the atmosphere through an air gap and must never be submerged or installed in a pit subject to flooding.

Official Citations

  • American Society of Sanitary Engineering (ASSE): Standards 1013, 1015, 1020, 1001.
  • International Code Council (ICC): IPC Section 608.13 through 608.16 (Backflow prevention).
  • International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO): UPC Section 603.5 (Specific Devices for Backflow Prevention).
