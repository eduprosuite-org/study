What is a Backwater Valve?

Curator: John Masterson | Master Plumber
Fact Checked: Active
Updated: 2026-08-16
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A backwater valve is a mechanical backflow prevention device installed in a building's sanitary drainage piping. It is designed to automatically close when waste flows backward from the public sewer main, shielding low-level basements and fixtures from sewage backups.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Backwater valves operate using a floating flap, gate, or ball mechanism. Under normal conditions, gravity and outgoing water pressure push the flap open, allowing wastewater to discharge freely into the municipal sewer line. However, if the municipal main surcharges (due to heavy rain, flooding, or blockages) and pushes wastewater backward into the lateral line, the rising water lifts the buoyant flap, sealing the gate shut against its seat. This blocks sewage from entering the low-level building drains.

A critical concept is that a backwater valve must **only** protect fixtures located below the level of the next upstream manhole cover. If fixtures on upper floors are routed through the backwater valve, and the valve closes during a sewer backup, any water used on upper floors will have nowhere to go and will flood the basement from the inside. Therefore, upper-level drains must bypass the backwater valve and connect downstream of it.

Code & Technical Regulations

Both codes strictly dictate when and where backwater valves must be installed, focusing heavily on fixture elevations relative to the sewer network:

Requirement IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Mandatory Installation Section 715.1: Required for fixtures with flood rims below the elevation of the next upstream manhole cover. Section 710.1: Required for fixtures with flood level rims below the elevation of the next upstream manhole or street curb.
Permitted Fixtures on Valve Branch Section 715.1: Only fixtures below the upstream manhole level may connect to the backwater valve branch. Section 710.4: Upper floor fixtures must not discharge through the backwater valve.
Accessibility & Cover Section 715.4: Must be accessible for inspection and cleaning. Section 710.3: Must have an accessible access cover.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

  • Upstream Manhole Rule: On both the Journeyman Plumber Exam and the Master Contractor Exam, expect scenarios where you must identify if a backwater valve is needed. The key reference point is whether the flood level rim of the fixture is below the elevation of the next **upstream manhole cover**.
  • Self-Flooding Hazard: Be prepared for questions asking why upper floor fixtures must not drain into the backwater valve. If the valve closes, upper floor usage will flood lower levels.
  • Maintenance Access: Always remember that backwater valves require access. Installing a valve under a poured concrete slab without an access hatch or sleeve is a critical code violation.

Official Citations
