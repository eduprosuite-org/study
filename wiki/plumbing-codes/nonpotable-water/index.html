What is Nonpotable Water?

Curator: John Masterson
Fact-Checked & Updated: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
Water that is not safe for drinking, personal use, or culinary purposes, but can be utilized for flushing, irrigation, or industrial applications.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Nonpotable water systems are increasingly common in green building designs to reduce municipal water consumption. Nonpotable water includes graywater, rainwater, reclaimed municipal wastewater, and industrial process water. While this water is highly useful for toilet flushing, landscape irrigation, cooling towers, and fire protection, it presents a serious public health hazard if it is accidentally mixed with the potable (drinking) water system. A single cross-connection can contaminate an entire building's water supply.

To prevent cross-connection, plumbing codes require absolute separation of systems. Nonpotable water piping must be painted or wrapped in purple. All outlets (faucets, valves) must be clearly labeled with warnings such as 'CAUTION: NONPOTABLE WATER, DO NOT DRINK'. Backflow prevention assemblies, such as reduced pressure zone (RPZ) devices or physical air gaps, are mandatory where potable lines supplement nonpotable systems.

Code & Technical Regulations

Navigating plumbing standards requires understanding specific code differences. Here is a comparison of design parameters between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Parameter IPC Regulation UPC Regulation
Piping Color and Labeling Purple color (PANTONE 512C or equivalent) or purple wrap with text every 5 feet (Section 608.8) Purple piping and text labeled 'NONPOTABLE WATER' every 5 feet (Section 601.3)
Cross-Connection Protection Requires physical air gap or RPZ backflow preventer (Section 1301.5) Requires physical air gap or reduced pressure backflow assembly (Section 1502.2)
Rainwater System Sizing Based on local rainfall rates and roof area (Section 1303) Based on detailed regional tables and engineering criteria (Chapter 16)

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Understanding how licensing exams test this topic is critical for passing. Below are the key focus areas for licensing candidates:

  • Journeyman Plumber Exam Focus: Pipes carrying nonpotable water must be purple, and all outlets must have warning signs. A cross-connection is the link between potable and nonpotable systems. Test your knowledge with practice exams on the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep page and utilize the General Free Prep sections.
  • Master Plumber & Contractor Focus: Understand high-hazard vs low-hazard backflow prevention. Master contractors must design makeup water systems with correct air gaps to prevent nonpotable backflow. Review complex system layouts and administrative oversight on the Master Contractor Exam Prep page.
  • Tradesman & Inspector Focus: Perform pressure tests and cross-connection checks (shut off potable water and verify nonpotable lines still have pressure) to ensure separation. Prepare for field layout questions and regulatory compliance scenarios with the resources on the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.
