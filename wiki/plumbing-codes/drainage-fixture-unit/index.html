What is a Drainage Fixture Unit (DFU)?

Curator: John Masterson | Master Plumber
Fact Checked: Active
Updated: 2026-08-16
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A Drainage Fixture Unit (DFU) is a dimensionless mathematical value assigned to plumbing fixtures to represent their load-producing effect on the wastewater system. It is calculated based on flow rate, run duration, and frequency of use, allowing plumbers to size pipes using cumulative sums.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Sizing a plumbing drainage system based solely on the maximum flow rate of every fixture running simultaneously would lead to massive, inefficient pipes. To solve this, Dr. Roy B. Hunter of the National Bureau of Standards developed the fixture unit concept in the 1940s. By applying probability theory (known as Hunter's Curve), he calculated the likelihood of multiple fixtures discharging at the exact same moment in a building.

The baseline unit of 1.0 DFU is historically based on the discharge rate of a standard residential lavatory (approx. 7.5 gallons per minute, or 1 cubic foot per minute). All other fixtures are assigned DFU values relative to this baseline. For instance, a water closet dumps a large volume of water in a brief surge, giving it a higher value (3.0 or 4.0 DFUs). Conversely, a continuous-flow device (like an ejector pump or AC condensate line) must be converted using a gpm-to-DFU formula: 1 GPM of continuous flow is equivalent to 2 DFUs.

Code & Technical Regulations

Both model plumbing codes list fixture unit values in Chapter 7, with minor differences in water closet and pipe limits:

Fixture / Sizing Limit IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Private Water Closet (Gravity Toilet) 3.0 DFUs (Table 709.1) 3.0 DFUs (Table 702.1)
Public Water Closet (Flushometer) 4.0 DFUs 4.0 DFUs
3" Horizontal Branch Limit Table 710.1(1): Max 20 DFUs (no toilet limits on the branch). Table 703.2: Max 20 DFUs (but a maximum of 2 water closets may connect to a 3" line).
4" Sewer at 1/4" slope Table 710.1(1): Max 216 DFUs. Table 703.2: Max 216 DFUs.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

  • Memorize Common DFUs: On the Journeyman Plumber Exam, memorize: Lavatory = 1 DFU, Kitchen Sink = 2 DFU, Shower = 2 DFU, Bathtub = 2 DFU, Residential Toilet = 3 DFU.
  • Continuous Flow Conversion: On the Master Contractor Exam, you will see questions like: "An air conditioning unit discharges 15 GPM of continuous condensate water. How many DFUs must be added to the drain sizing?" The formula is: **DFU = GPM × 2**. The answer is 30 DFUs.
  • Sewer Sizing: Practice accumulating DFUs down a riser diagram and sizing the main building drain and building sewer at different slopes (1/16, 1/8, or 1/4 inch per foot).
  • For pipe sizing calculators and practice riser diagrams, check our General Free Prep Portal and state study aids at the Tradesman & Inspector page.

Official Citations

  • National Bureau of Standards (NBS): BMS65 ("Methods of Estimating Loads in Plumbing Systems" by Roy B. Hunter).
  • International Code Council (ICC): IPC Section 709 (Fixture Connections) & Section 710 (Sizing).
  • International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO): UPC Section 702 (Fixture Unit Values) & Section 703 (Size of Drainage Piping).
