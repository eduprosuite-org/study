Trade Term Dictionary

What is Stack Vent?

Curator: John Masterson
Fact-Checked & Updated: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
The extension of a soil or waste stack above the highest horizontal drain connection to the stack, terminating in the open air.

Core Concept & Mechanism

A stack vent (often confused with a vent stack) is the vertical extension of a soil or waste stack above the highest horizontal drainage connection. Because it is a continuation of the waste stack, it does not carry liquid waste itself but instead serves as the primary vent for the drainage stack. The stack vent allows air to flow freely into the stack, balancing the vacuum created as waste flows down. It must run as directly as possible through the roof to terminate in the open air.

In contrast, a vent stack is a dedicated vertical vent pipe installed solely to provide air circulation to and from the drainage system, connecting to the waste stack near its base. Sizing of the stack vent is based on the total DFU load on the entire stack and the developed length of the vent. The termination of the stack vent through the roof must be protected from freezing in cold climates by increasing its diameter (typically to 3 inches) before it passes through the roof.

Code & Technical Regulations

Navigating plumbing standards requires understanding specific code differences. Here is a comparison of design parameters between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Parameter IPC Regulation UPC Regulation
Main Vent Requirement Every building must have at least one vent stack or stack vent extending to the open air (Section 903.1) Every building must have at least one main stack vent extending to the open air (Section 903.1)
Sizing Basis Based on total DFU load and developed length (Section 906.1) Based on total DFU load and developed length (Section 904.1)
Frost Closure Protection Must be increased to a minimum of 3 inches at least 12 inches below the roof line (Section 903.2) Must be increased to a minimum of 3 inches at least 10 inches below the roof line (Section 906.7)
