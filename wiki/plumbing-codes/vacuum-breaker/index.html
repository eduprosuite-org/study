What is Vacuum Breaker?

Curator: John Masterson
Fact-Checked & Updated: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A backflow preventer that introduces air into a water line to break a siphon, preventing backsiphonage of contaminated water.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Vacuum breakers are essential safety devices used to protect the potable water supply from back-siphonage. Back-siphonage occurs when a drop in supply pressure creates a vacuum, sucking contaminated water backward into the system. An atmospheric vacuum breaker (AVB) contains a check valve that rises when water flows, sealing an air inlet. When flow stops or pressure drops, the check valve falls, opening the air inlet to break the siphon. AVBs cannot be subjected to continuous pressure.

A pressure vacuum breaker (PVB) can be under continuous pressure. It contains an independently operating check valve loaded by a spring, and an air inlet valve loaded by a spring. PVBs are commonly used on irrigation systems and must be installed at least 12 inches above the highest outlet. Both types must be installed vertically and cannot be concealed. Annual testing is required for PVBs to ensure the springs and seals are functioning.

Code & Technical Regulations

Navigating plumbing standards requires understanding specific code differences. Here is a comparison of design parameters between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Parameter IPC Regulation UPC Regulation
AVB Installation Height Minimum 6 inches above the flood level rim of the highest outlet (Section 608.15.4) Minimum 6 inches above the flood level rim of the highest outlet (Section 603.5.6)
PVB Installation Height Minimum 12 inches above the highest point of discharge (Section 608.15.4) Minimum 12 inches above the highest point of discharge (Section 603.5.6)
Continuous Pressure Limit AVB: No continuous pressure (max 12 hours out of 24); PVB: Permitted AVB: No continuous pressure; PVB: Permitted (Section 603.5.6)

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Understanding how licensing exams test this topic is critical for passing. Below are the key focus areas for licensing candidates:

  • Journeyman Plumber Exam Focus: AVBs cannot have shutoff valves downstream and must be 6 inches above the rim. PVBs must be 12 inches above and can have shutoffs. Test your knowledge with practice exams on the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep page and utilize the General Free Prep sections.
  • Master Plumber & Contractor Focus: Learn how to select backflow preventers for high-hazard cross-connections. PVBs are for backsiphonage only, not backpressure. Review complex system layouts and administrative oversight on the Master Contractor Exam Prep page.
  • Tradesman & Inspector Focus: Verify that vacuum breakers are installed vertically and are accessible for testing. Check for leaks around the air vents. Prepare for field layout questions and regulatory compliance scenarios with the resources on the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.
