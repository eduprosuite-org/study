What is an Accumulator?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Updated: August 16, 2026
Verified & Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
An accumulator (or pressure tank) is a pressurized storage vessel used in water distribution and booster systems to store energy in the form of compressed air or a spring mechanism. It maintains system pressure, minimizes pump cycling, and mitigates pressure fluctuations.

Core Concept & Mechanism

In plumbing and water supply engineering, an accumulator (pressure tank) operates on the principle of compressibility. While water is virtually incompressible, air can be easily compressed. The tank typically contains a flexible rubber bladder or diaphragm separating a pre-charged chamber of compressed air from the water storage chamber. When the water booster pump runs, it forces water into the accumulator, compressing the air chamber behind the bladder. Once the system reaches its high-pressure cutoff limit, the pump shuts off.

As fixtures are opened and water is drawn, the compressed air expands, pushing the water out of the bladder and maintaining system pressure. This prevents the booster pump from short-cycling (turning on and off rapidly for minor water draws), which extends pump motor life and reduces energy usage. Sizing an accumulator requires calculating the pump's minimum runtime, the system's design flow rate (GPM), and the differential pressure switch settings (cut-in/cut-out pressures).

Code & Technical Regulations

Booster pumps, pressure tanks, and thermal expansion chambers are highly regulated by model plumbing codes to prevent water distribution failure, structural hazards, and cross-connections.

Technical Parameter International Plumbing Code (IPC) Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC)
Booster Pump Controls Section 606.5.1: Booster systems must maintain minimum flow pressure and have automatic low-pressure shutoffs (set ≥ 10 PSI). Section 608.2: Pressure tanks must have safety relief valves to prevent exceeding maximum pressure ratings.
Thermal Expansion Control Section 607.3: Required in closed water systems to limit pressure rise to no more than the safety relief valve setting. Section 608.3: A thermal expansion tank or equivalent device is mandatory in any closed water heating system.
Pressure Vessel Certification Section 606.5.2: Tanks over specific sizes must be constructed and stamped in accordance with ASME BPVC. Section 608.2: Expansion and accumulator vessels must comply with recognized NSF/ANSI 61 and ASME standards.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Exams test candidates on sizing booster pumps and accumulator tanks to maintain system pressures:

  • Booster Pump Sizing: Practice calculations relating to system head, fixture unit load (WSFUs), and minimum pressures. Review these math concepts inside the Journeyman Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Thermal Expansion Tank Sizing: Understand the differences between expansion tanks and standard accumulators. Memorize the codes governing water hammer arrestors and closed-loop pressure relief. Study layout constraints under the Master Contractor Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Tank Pre-charge Settings: Know that the pre-charge air pressure in a bladder tank should be set at 2 PSI below the pump cut-in pressure (e.g., 28 PSI pre-charge for a 30-50 PSI pressure switch). Study these mechanical facts inside the General & Free Prep Portal.
  • Low-pressure Cutoffs: The IPC requires a low-pressure cutoff switch on water pressure booster systems to prevent suction-line vacuum. Practice these inspector-level codes at the Tradesman & Inspector Exams.
