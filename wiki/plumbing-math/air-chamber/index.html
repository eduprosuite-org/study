What is an Air Chamber?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Updated: August 16, 2026
Verified & Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
An air chamber is a vertical, capped section of pipe installed near fast-acting valves in water supply systems. It traps air to act as a cushion, absorbing the hydraulic shock (water hammer) created when the water flow is suddenly stopped.

Core Concept & Mechanism

When water flows through a pipe, it possesses kinetic energy. When a fast-closing valve (such as a solenoid valve on a washing machine or dishwasher, or a quick-shut single-lever faucet) closes instantly, the moving water column is brought to an abrupt halt. This kinetic energy converts into a high-pressure shock wave—ranging from 300 to over 600 PSI—that travels backward through the pipe, producing a loud pounding noise known as water hammer. Over time, this hydraulic shock damages pipes, joints, hangers, and fixtures.

An air chamber works by trapping a pocket of air at the highest point near the valve. Because air is compressible and water is not, the shock wave compresses the trapped air, which acts like a shock absorber, neutralizing the kinetic energy. However, field-fabricated air chambers eventually fail because the trapped air dissolves into the water over time (waterlogging). Modern plumbing systems require engineered water hammer arrestors, which use a sealed mechanical piston or bladder to permanently separate the air charge from the water.

Code & Technical Regulations

Model plumbing codes mandate the control of water hammer, transitioning away from field-fabricated air chambers to engineered water hammer arrestors.

Technical Parameter International Plumbing Code (IPC) Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC)
Mandatory Water Hammer Protection Section 606.6: Must be installed where quick-acting valves are utilized (dishwasher, washing machine, flushometers). Section 609.10: Required on all water supply systems where quick-acting valves are installed.
Fabricated Air Chambers Section 606.6: Not recognized as compliant; engineered water hammer arrestors must be used. Section 609.10.1: Allowed only when approved by the AHJ and provided with an accessible means of recharging.
Compliance Standards Section 606.6: Must comply with ASSE 1010 (Water Hammer Arrestors). Section 609.10: Must conform to ASSE 1010 or PDI-WH 201.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Exams regularly test candidates on identifying the causes of water hammer and the correct applications of arrestors:

  • ASSE 1010 standard: Memorize this standard number. Licensing exams frequently ask which standard governs water hammer arrestors. Practice this inside the Journeyman Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Arrestor Sizing: Master candidates must know how to size arrestors using PDI-WH 201 fixture units (Sizes A, B, C, D, E, F). Check our sizing guides at the Master Contractor Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Why Air Chambers Fail: Be prepared to explain that air chambers lose their cushion due to air absorption into the water. Review physical plumbing math concepts in the General & Free Prep Portal.
  • Quick-closing Valves: Identify which fixtures require protection (dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers). Study inspector checklist details under the Tradesman & Inspector Exams.
