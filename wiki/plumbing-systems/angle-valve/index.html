What is an Angle Valve?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
An angle valve (often called an angle stop) is a manually operated shut-off valve with its inlet and outlet ports oriented at a 90-degree angle. It is used to control and isolate the water supply to individual plumbing fixtures like sinks, water closets, and washing machines.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Angle valves are critical mechanical components designed to facilitate local water isolation without shutting down the entire water distribution system of a building. When a fixture requires service or leaks, the angle stop shuts off flow locally.

The internal mechanism of an angle valve can be a multi-turn compression stem (which relies on a rubber washer compressed against an internal seat) or a modern quarter-turn ball design (utilizing a brass ball with a hole through it rotating inside Teflon seats). The 90-degree turn of the body makes it highly effective for connecting pipes that stub out from walls, as it redirects the water flow cleanly upward to the fixture's faucet or flush tank.

These valves are typically constructed of forged brass, chrome-plated brass, or engineered plastics. Inlet connections include female iron pipe (FIP) threads, solder joint (sweat) ports, compression collars, or push-to-connect (such as SharkBite) ports, while the outlet is commonly a compression thread designed to accept a 3/8-inch or 1/2-inch flexible riser tube.

Code & Technical Regulations

Plumbing codes require dedicated fixture shut-off valves in most installations to ensure maintenance accessibility and minimize water damage during emergencies.

Requirement Type IPC Section 606.2 UPC Section 606.2 / 606.3
Individual Shut-off Requirement Required on all fixtures except in residential occupancies if main shutoff and manifold isolation are provided. Required at all fixtures. Shall be located on the fixture supply pipe in an accessible location.
Access to Valve Must be "accessible" (removable panels or open exposure allowed). Must be "accessible" and within the same room as the fixture served.
Public Facilities Exception Allows key-operated stops or valves inside chase walls. Valves must be tamper-resistant or located in utility chases.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Test administrators frequently design questions around valve placement:

  • Required Locations: Be prepared for scenario questions about which fixtures can share a valve or omit a valve. Under both codes, bathtubs and showers do not require separate stops if there is a main shut-off valve, but sinks and water closets always require individual shut-offs under UPC. Prepare with the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Accessibility Definitions: Understand the difference between "accessible" (having access but may require removing a panel) and "readily accessible" (direct access without tools or removing panels). Valves must be accessible. Review this difference in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Slip Joints vs. Compression Joints: Make sure you know where slip joints are permitted downstream of shutoff valves and the restrictions on pipe connection materials. Review the general plumbing material sections in General Free Prep or the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.

Official Citations

  • IAPMO UPC Section 606.2: Specifies installation standards and accessibility requirements for water service shut-off valves.
