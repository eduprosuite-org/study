Trade Term Dictionary

What is a Horizontal Branch?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A horizontal branch is a drainage pipe that extends laterally from a soil-and-waste stack. It receives wastewater from individual fixture drains on a single floor, conveying the collected load to the vertical drainage stack.

Core Concept & Mechanism

In multi-story buildings, vertical soil and waste stacks run downward to the building drain. To gather waste from plumbing fixtures on each individual floor, horizontal drainage pipelines must be installed. These lateral collection lines are known as horizontal branches.

To keep solids suspended and prevent deposits, horizontal branches must be installed with a continuous slope (typically 1/4 inch per foot for pipes under 3 inches). Sizing is dictated by calculating the cumulative Drainage Fixture Units (DFUs) draining into the branch on that level.

Proper venting is required along the branch to protect fixture trap seals. For commercial layouts, circuit venting is commonly employed, where a single horizontal branch serves up to 8 fixtures (such as toilets) with a single relief loop vent connected upstream.

Code & Technical Regulations

Codes specify the maximum DFU loads allowed on a single horizontal branch based on pipe diameter and slope.

Branch Diameter IPC Max DFU Capacity (Table 710.1(1)) UPC Max DFU Capacity (Table 703.2)
2 inches Max 6 DFUs (pitched at 1/4" per foot) Max 8 DFUs (pitched at 1/4" per foot)
3 inches Max 20 DFUs (pitched at 1/8" or 1/4" per foot) Max 35 DFUs (pitched at 1/4" per foot)
4 inches Max 160 DFUs (pitched at 1/8" or 1/4" per foot) Max 216 DFUs (pitched at 1/4" per foot)

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

DWV horizontal branch sizing is a major focus on both levels of trade exams:

  • DFU Calculations: You will be shown a multi-fixture layout and asked to determine the size of a horizontal branch. Sum the DFU values of all connected fixtures, then select the minimum size using the code tables. Study this in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Circuit Venting Rules: A horizontal branch serving up to 8 fixtures can be circuit vented. The circuit vent must connect between the two most upstream fixtures on the branch. Study these layouts in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Offset Prohibitions: Gaps and connections are prohibited near offset angles at the base of stacks. Make sure you understand the vertical-to-horizontal clearance zones. Review offsets in General Free Prep or Tradesman & Inspector Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 202 & Table 710.1(1): Structural definitions and sizing limits for horizontal branch lines.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 703.0 & Table 703.2: Outlines drainage capacity and slope rules for lateral branches.
  • ASME A112.14.3: Standards for drainage equipment and interceptor fittings.
