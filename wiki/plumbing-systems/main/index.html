What is Main?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Last Updated: August 16, 2026
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A main is the principal pipe run in a plumbing system to which branch lines are connected, serving as the central highway for supply or drainage distribution.

Core Concept & Mechanism

In plumbing systems, the main acts as the central arterial conduit. For water distribution, the water main carries the highest volume of water and the full system pressure from the utility entry point or booster pumps. For sanitary systems, the building drain main represents the lowest horizontal drainage piping that collects the discharge of all waste, soil, and vent stacks within the building's footprint, directing it out to the building sewer. In both scenarios, the main must be sized to handle the cumulative hydraulic load of all connected branches without experiencing excessive friction loss, pressure drops, or velocity spikes that could cause water hammer, structural vibration, or drainage backup.

Engineering a main involves calculating the total drainage fixture units (DFUs) or water supply fixture units (WSFUs) to determine the volumetric flow rate. The velocity of water in supply mains is typically limited to 5 to 8 feet per second (fps) to protect the pipe walls from erosion-corrosion and prevent hydraulic shock. In contrast, drainage mains must maintain a minimum velocity of 2 feet per second (fps) to achieve a self-scouring action, ensuring that suspended solids do not settle and create blockages. Changes in direction along a drainage main must be executed with sweep fittings rather than short 90-degree elbows to maintain this laminar flow.

Code & Technical Regulations

Proper installation of Main requires strict adherence to building and plumbing codes. Sizing, material compatibility, and clearance rules vary between jurisdictions. The table below outlines the primary regulatory differences between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Topic IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Drainage Main Sizing Sized based on Table 710.1(1) using total DFUs. A 3-inch horizontal building drain main at 1/4" slope can carry up to 36 DFUs. Sized based on Table 703.2. A 3-inch horizontal drainage main at 1/4" slope is limited to a maximum of 35 DFUs.
Water Supply Velocity Section 604.1 limits velocity based on pipe manufacturer specifications, typically recommending a maximum of 8 fps for cold water. Section 608.1 limits flow velocities in water distribution systems to a maximum of 10 fps, though copper is often limited to 5-8 fps.
Drainage Pipe Slope Section 704.1 requires horizontal drainage mains 2 inches or less to slope at 1/4" per foot; 3 to 6 inches can slope at 1/8" per foot. Section 718.1 requires drainage pipes less than 4 inches to slope at 1/4" per foot. Sizing adjustments require administrative approval.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Licensing examinations frequently test candidates on code requirements, sizing methodologies, and terminology associated with Main. Master these key insights to prepare:

  • When sizing horizontal building drain mains, always cross-reference the minimum required slope. Under-sloped mains are a major source of system failure and are heavily tested on both the Journeyman Exam and the Master Contractor Exam.
  • Calculate the capacity of water service and distribution mains using fixture unit demand charts. Understand how to convert fixture counts into WSFUs and read friction loss curves for different piping materials in the General Free Prep.
  • Inspectors and plan reviewers frequently test candidates on their ability to locate cleanouts. A cleanout must be provided at the connection of the building drain main to the building sewer, and at any change of direction greater than 45 degrees, as detailed in the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.
