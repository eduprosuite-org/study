What is an Ejector Pump?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A sewage ejector pump is a motor-driven system designed to lift domestic wastewater and raw sewage from below-grade plumbing fixtures (like basements) up into the primary gravity-flow building sewer.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Sewage ejector systems are required whenever plumbing fixtures are located below the level of the main gravity sewer line serving the building. Because gravity cannot draw waste upward, fixtures in basements or lower cellars drain horizontally into a sealed, sub-slab sump basin.

As waste levels inside the sump basin rise, a float switch triggers a submersible centrifugal pump. The pump features a specialized open-vane impeller designed to handle suspended solids up to 2 inches in diameter. Grinder pumps are a variation that shreds solid waste into a fine slurry, permitting smaller discharge piping runs.

To prevent sewer gases from entering living areas, the sump basin must be sealed with an airtight, gas-tight gasketed cover and connected directly to the building's vent stack. The discharge line requires a check valve (to stop pumped liquid from draining back into the pit) and a gate valve (to isolate the line during maintenance).

Code & Technical Regulations

The plumbing codes enforce strict rules on sump venting, check valve order, and solids handling capabilities to ensure safe system operation.

Requirement Item IPC Regulations (Section 712) UPC Regulations (Section 710)
Minimum Vent Size Min 1-1/4 inches (sized based on GPM flow and run length) (Section 712.3.1). Min 1-1/2 inches (sized based on drainage fixture unit load) (Section 710.10).
Discharge Pipe Size Min 2 inches for solids-handling pumps (Section 712.2). Min 2 inches for solids-handling pumps (Section 710.3).
Valve Configuration Requires check valve and full-port gate or ball valve on discharge line (Section 712.1). Requires check valve and full-way gate valve on discharge line, located on discharge side of check valve (Section 710.4).

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Sump and ejector systems are heavily featured on licensing examinations due to safety concerns:

  • Valve Installation Order: Pay close attention to valve order. On exams, remember the check valve is closest to the pump, and the gate/isolation valve is installed downstream. Prepare for these troubleshooting questions in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Basin Cover Seals: Basement ejector pits must have gas-tight lids. Standard sump pumps (for groundwater) do not require gas-tight seals, but sewage ejector pits always do. Verify these differences with the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • DFU Loading: Understand how a running pump adds load to the building drain. A pump is assigned a drainage fixture unit load based on GPM discharge rate (commonly 2 DFUs per GPM). Study pump loads in General Free Prep or Tradesman & Inspector Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 712: Complete regulations for design, venting, and valves of sumps and ejectors.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 710.0: Sewer and sump pump design standards and valve placement.
  • UL 778: Standard for motor-operated water and wastewater pumps.
