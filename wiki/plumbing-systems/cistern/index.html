What is a Cistern?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A cistern is a gravity-fed, non-pressurized tank used to collect and store large volumes of rainwater or potable well water. Commonly located underground, at grade, or on roofs, cisterns play a key role in greywater reuse, irrigation, and rainwater harvesting.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Cisterns are static storage reservoirs that gather water from a supply source and save it for residential, agricultural, or commercial consumption. In modern rainwater harvesting layouts, water from roof gutters passes through a debris filter and a first-flush diverter (which redirects the initial, dirty rainfall containing dust and bird droppings away from the tank). The pre-filtered water then enters the cistern.

To prevent water stagnation and anaerobic conditions, cisterns incorporate smooth water inlets (calmed inlets) that do not stir up sediment at the bottom. They also feature screened vents to allow air exchange, and gravity-drained overflow ports to direct excess rainwater away safely.

If a cistern is designed to hold potable water, the tank material must conform to strict food-grade standards (such as NSF/ANSI 61). It must also be constructed of opaque materials or buried underground to block sunlight, which prevents the growth of photosynthetic algae.

Code & Technical Regulations

Plumbing codes require strict barriers between stored nonpotable water and potable supply lines to prevent backflow and cross-contamination.

Regulation Topic IPC Chapter 13 Standards UPC Chapter 16 Standards
Cross-Connection Control Must be isolated from potable supply by an approved air gap or mechanical backflow preventer (Section 1301.9). Air gap required for backup potable water supply connections. No direct cross-connections permitted (Section 1602.3).
Water Treatment & Dye Nonpotable water for flushing toilets must be dyed blue or green or disinfected (Section 1303.1). Requires filtration, disinfection, and regular water quality testing for reuse applications (Section 1602.5).
Labeling Requirements Pipes must be color-coded purple or wrapped with purple identification tape (Section 1301.3). Requires purple piping and clear stamps stating "CAUTION: NONPOTABLE WATER - DO NOT DRINK" (Section 1602.9).

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Cistern and rainwater containment regulations are tested to ensure public health safety:

  • Air Gap Dimensions: Know how to calculate the minimum air gap size when a municipal water pipe serves as a backup supply for a cistern. The gap must be at least twice the diameter of the supply outlet, but never less than 1 inch. Prepare with the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Purple Pipe Rule: Remember that all nonpotable water supply lines (such as reclaimed cistern lines supplying toilets or irrigation) must be colored purple. Study state-specific water reuse laws in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Overflow Sizing: Cistern overflow outlets must not be smaller than the inlet supply pipes. Learn the hydraulic math for sizing large-volume storage overflows in General Free Prep or the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 1301 & 1303: Standards for nonpotable water systems, storage tanks, and disinfection.
  • IAPMO UPC Chapter 16: Detailed requirements for alternate water source systems and irrigation.
  • NSF/ANSI Standard 350: Evaluates onsite residential and commercial water reuse systems.
