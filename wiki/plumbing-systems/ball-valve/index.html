What is a Ball Valve?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A ball valve is a quarter-turn, rotary shut-off valve that utilizes a spherical, bored-through ball to regulate or block the flow of liquids or gases. They are preferred in plumbing systems for their reliable, leak-free seals, rapid operation, and minimal flow resistance.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Ball valves represent one of the most reliable and widely used valve designs in water distribution, gas lines, and industrial plumbing systems. Inside the valve, a spherical ball sits between two sealing rings or "seats" (typically constructed of PTFE, or Teflon). The ball has a cylindrical hole or bore running through its center.

When the valve handle is aligned parallel to the pipeline, the bore is oriented in line with the pipe, allowing water to flow through. When the handle is turned 90 degrees (quarter-turn), the ball rotates so the solid faces are exposed to the flow, completely sealing off the stream.

Unlike globe or gate valves, ball valves offer immediate visual confirmation of status (handle parallel means open; handle perpendicular means closed). In plumbing applications, "full-port" ball valves are standard. They feature a bore diameter equal to the pipe's internal diameter, resulting in zero friction loss and preventing sediment build-up.

Code & Technical Regulations

Both the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) heavily regulate shut-off valves, particularly at the main building entrance and water heating equipment.

Requirement Type IPC Section (Section 606) UPC Section (Section 606)
Water Service Main Valve Must be a full-open valve (Section 606.1). Ball valves are approved. Must be a full-way valve (Section 606.1). Ball valves meeting standard are required.
Valves on Water Heaters Requires a shutoff valve on the cold water supply pipe (Section 606.2). Requires a full-way shutoff valve on the cold water supply pipe (Section 606.2).
Approval Standard Must conform to MSS SP-110, ASME B16.44, or NSF 61 (potable). Must conform to IAPMO PS 117 or listed equivalents.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Ball valves are frequently featured on regulatory exam questions due to their hydraulic properties and use as main shutoffs:

  • Full-Open / Full-Way Valve Rule: Main water distribution entry valves must be full-port/full-open. Be sure to select "full-open ball valve" over globe or needle valves in multiple-choice questions. Prepare for these questions in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Pressure Drop and Friction Loss: Full-port ball valves introduce negligible pressure drops. When calculating Equivalent Length (EL) of fittings for pipe sizing, a ball valve has a very low coefficient compared to a globe valve. Review these sizing math tables in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Gas Service Isolations: Ball valves are also code-approved for gas line isolations (shut-off stops for fireplaces, furnaces, and water heaters). Learn the pressure ratings and testing rules for gas stops in General Free Prep or the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 606.1: Defines where full-open valves are mandatory on water service lines.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 606.1 & Table 604.1: Lists approved valves and certifications for potable supply.
  • MSS SP-110: Industry standard for ball valves with threaded, socket-welding, solder joint, or grooved ends.
