What is Soil Stack?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Last Updated: August 16, 2026
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
The primary vertical drainage pipe that collects discharge from water closets (toilets) and other fixtures and conveys it to the building drain.

Core Concept & Mechanism

The soil stack is the vertical trunk line of the sanitary drainage system. It runs vertically through multiple floors, collecting horizontal branch lines from toilet groups and other fixtures. The vertical alignment must remain as straight as possible. If an offset is necessary (shifting the stack horizontally), it creates severe turbulence and pressure surges. In multi-story buildings, offsets in soil stacks require specialized relief vents to protect fixture traps on lower levels.

The soil stack must extend vertically through the roof to the open air. The portion of the stack above the highest horizontal branch connection is called the stack vent. This stack vent acts as an air relief conduit, allowing air to enter the stack as wastewater falls, balancing pressure and protecting the water seals in all connected fixture traps.

Code & Technical Regulations

Proper installation of Soil Stack requires strict adherence to building and plumbing codes. Sizing, material compatibility, and clearance rules vary between jurisdictions. The table below outlines the primary regulatory differences between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Topic IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Stack Vent Termination Section 903.1 requires stack vents to extend at least 6 inches above the roof (or higher if subject to snow). Section 906.1 requires vents to terminate at least 6 inches above the roof and 10 feet away from any window or air intake.
Stack Sizing Table 710.1(2) sizes stacks based on total DFUs and stack height. A 3-inch stack can carry up to 72 DFUs. Table 703.2 sizes stacks based on height and DFUs. A 3-inch stack is limited to 48 DFUs.
Multi-story Offsets Section 711 allows offsets of 45 degrees or less without relief vents in stacks of fewer than 5 branch intervals. Section 711.0 requires relief vents for offsets in drainage stacks of five or more stories.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Licensing examinations frequently test candidates on code requirements, sizing methodologies, and terminology associated with Soil Stack. Master these key insights to prepare:

  • Understand stack vent termination requirements. In cold regions, vents must be protected from frost closure by increasing the pipe size to at least 3 inches. Study these rules at the Journeyman Exam Prep.
  • Master exams cover relief vent sizing for multi-story soil stacks. A relief vent is required for stacks with more than 10 branch intervals. Review these layout rules in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Learn to identify stack vents versus vent stacks in schematic diagrams. Try practice drawing modules in the General Free Prep.
