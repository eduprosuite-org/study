What is Tee Fitting?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Last Updated: August 16, 2026
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A T-shaped pipe fitting with three ports, allowing a branch line to connect to a run pipe at a 90-degree angle.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Tee fittings are used to split or combine flow. In pressure piping (water supply), tees are straight (90 degrees). However, in DWV (Drain, Waste, and Vent) systems, flow direction is critical to prevent waste buildup. Plumbers must use sanitary tees (which have a swept, curved branch) rather than straight tees. Under plumbing codes, the orientation of a sanitary tee is strictly regulated; it can only transition flow from horizontal to vertical, or be used on vent lines. It cannot be used to connect a vertical drain to a horizontal drain or a horizontal drain to another horizontal drain (where a wye and eighth bend must be used instead).

A double sanitary tee (often called a sanitary cross) allows two branches to enter a single vertical stack at the same level. However, codes restrict the use of double sanitary tees to prevent waste from one side discharging across the stack into the opposite fixture's drain. For back-to-back water closets, a double wye or combination fitting is preferred to prevent this crossover.

Code & Technical Regulations

Proper installation of Tee Fitting requires strict adherence to building and plumbing codes. Sizing, material compatibility, and clearance rules vary between jurisdictions. The table below outlines the primary regulatory differences between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Topic IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Horizontal-to-Horizontal Table 706.3 prohibits the use of sanitary tees for horizontal-to-horizontal drainage changes of direction. Section 706.0 prohibits sanitary tees in horizontal drainage lines; wye fittings must be used instead.
Vertical-to-Horizontal Table 706.3 prohibits sanitary tees for vertical-to-horizontal drainage changes (e.g., at the base of a stack). Table 701.2 prohibits sanitary tees for vertical-to-horizontal drainage changes of direction.
Double Sanitary Tees Section 706.3 permits double sanitary tees for back-to-back fixtures, provided the branch is not receiving water closets or pump discharge. Section 704.2 restricts double sanitary tees to prevent crossover; blow-out toilets require double wyes.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Licensing examinations frequently test candidates on code requirements, sizing methodologies, and terminology associated with Tee Fitting. Master these key insights to prepare:

  • Sanitary tees are prohibited for vertical-to-horizontal transitions. A combination wye and eighth bend must be used at the base of a vertical stack. Practice fitting selection for the Journeyman Exam.
  • Understand the limits on double sanitary tees. They are prohibited for back-to-back water closets if they are of the blowout type, as waste can shoot across the stack. Study this for the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Review the symbols for tee fittings, sanitary tees, and crosses on plumbing drawings using the General Free Prep.

Official Citations

  • IAPMO: Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), Table 701.2 & Section 706.0 (Changes in Direction)
