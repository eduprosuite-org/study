What is Sump?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Last Updated: August 16, 2026
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A pit, basin, or tank located below the level of the gravity drainage system that collects wastewater, sewage, or groundwater, requiring mechanical pumping to lift it into the main sewer.

Core Concept & Mechanism

A sump system is necessary when plumbing fixtures or subsoil drains are located in basements or areas below the level of the street sewer. The sump pit (basin) serves as a collection reservoir. When the liquid level in the basin rises, a float switch activates a submersible pump (such as a sump pump for clearwater or an ejector pump for sewage). The pump lifts the water through a discharge pipe into the building's gravity drainage system.

The piping layout of a sump discharge is highly regulated. A check valve must be installed on the vertical discharge line to prevent pumped water from draining back into the basin when the pump shuts off. A shutoff valve (gate or ball valve) must be installed downstream of the check valve to allow for maintenance or pump replacement. Sump basins that receive sanitary waste must be airtight and vented to prevent sewer gas accumulation.

Code & Technical Regulations

Proper installation of Sump requires strict adherence to building and plumbing codes. Sizing, material compatibility, and clearance rules vary between jurisdictions. The table below outlines the primary regulatory differences between the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC):

Regulatory Topic IPC (International Plumbing Code) UPC (Uniform Plumbing Code)
Venting Requirements Section 712.3 requires sanitary sumps to be airtight and vented (minimum 1-1/4" vent). Section 710.7 requires sewage ejector sumps to be airtight and vented with a minimum 1-1/2" vent.
Valve Configuration Section 712.2 requires a check valve and full-port valve (gate or ball) on the pump discharge line. Section 710.4 requires a check valve and gate valve on the discharge line to prevent backflow and allow servicing.
Basin Construction Section 712.1 requires the sump basin to be not less than 18 inches in diameter and 24 inches deep. Section 710.8 requires sump basins to be constructed of concrete, metal, or other approved materials.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Licensing examinations frequently test candidates on code requirements, sizing methodologies, and terminology associated with Sump. Master these key insights to prepare:

  • The check valve must always be installed upstream of the shutoff valve on the pump discharge pipe. Memorize this sequence for the Journeyman Exam.
  • Master plumbers must calculate Total Dynamic Head (TDH) and pump capacity (GPM) to size ejector pumps. Study pump curves in the Master Contractor Prep.
  • Learn the difference between a sump pump (groundwater) and a sewage ejector pump (solids). Review these systems in our General Free Prep.
