What is a Flushometer?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A flushometer (or flushometer valve) is a pressurized, water-metering valve that discharges a predetermined volume of water directly from water supply lines to flush toilets or urinals, eliminating the need for a storage tank.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Flushometers are the standard flushing devices in commercial and institutional settings, where restrooms experience constant use. Unlike gravity toilets that require minutes to refill a storage tank, a flushometer draws water directly from a pressurized water service line and is immediately ready to flush again.

The valve operates using either a diaphragm or a piston:

  • Actuation: Tripping the handle vents water from an upper control chamber, dropping the internal pressure holding the valve closed.
  • Flush Cycle: Supply pressure pushes the diaphragm or piston upward, opening the main seat and allowing a high-volume rush of water to discharge into the fixture bowl.
  • Refill & Closure: Water slowly leaks back into the upper chamber through a tiny metering bypass hole. Once pressures equalize, the valve seat closes.

Because flushometers connect clean drinking water lines directly to sewer-connected waste basins, backflow protection is critical. Every flushometer must include an atmospheric vacuum breaker (AVB) on the discharge side to prevent waste from back-siphoning into the potable water mains during a supply pressure drop.

Code & Technical Regulations

Codes regulate supply line sizes, water consumption volumes, and vacuum breaker installation heights to ensure backflow protection and water conservation.

Requirement IPC Regulations (Chapters 4 & 6) UPC Regulations (Chapters 4 & 6)
Minimum Supply Line Size 1 inch for water closets; 3/4 inch for urinals (Section 604.5). 1-inch size for toilets; 3/4-inch size for urinals (Table 610.4).
Vacuum Breaker Height Must be installed at least 6 inches above the flood level rim of the fixture (Section 608.16.4). The critical level (CL) of the vacuum breaker must be at least 6 inches above the fixture (Section 603.5.7).
Max Water Consumption Toilets: 1.6 GPF. Urinals: 1.0 GPF (Table 604.4). Toilets: 1.6 GPF. Urinals: 1.0 GPF (Section 402.1).

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Flushometer hydraulics and backflow rules are heavily prioritized on licensing exams:

  • 6-Inch Vacuum Breaker Rule: Memorize the 6-inch clearance rule. On exams, locating a flushometer vacuum breaker closer than 6 inches to the toilet's spud inlet or flood rim is a major code violation. Study this in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Supply Pipe Sizing: Urinals require a 3/4-inch supply pipe, while water closets require a 1-inch supply pipe. Sizing smaller lines will cause flush failure. Practice sizing flushometer systems in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Water Pressure minimums: Flushometers require a higher minimum operating pressure than gravity toilets (typically 15 to 25 PSI running pressure at the valve). Study this in General Free Prep or Tradesman & Inspector Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 421.2 & 608.16.4: Sizing, flushing devices, and vacuum breaker standards.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 603.5.7 & 413.0: Backflow prevention and flushometer device requirements.
  • ASSE 1001 & ASSE 1037: Standards for atmospheric vacuum breakers and flushometer valves.
