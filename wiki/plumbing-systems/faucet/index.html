What is a Faucet?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A faucet is a plumbing valve assembly that controls the delivery of water to fixtures like sinks and tubs. Serving as the terminal device in water distribution lines, faucets regulate both volume flow and temperature.

Core Concept & Mechanism

Faucets regulate pressurized water delivery to residential and commercial fixtures. Under typical supply line pressure (40 to 80 PSI), hot and cold water feeds enter the base of the faucet. The internal valving mechanism controls the blending and volume of the water.

Valving technology has evolved through four main design formats:

  • Compression Valves: Uses a threaded stem to compress a rubber washer against a seat. (prone to wear and leaks).
  • Ball Valves: Uses a slotted metal or plastic sphere to adjust flow pathways (common in single-handle designs).
  • Cartridge Valves: Uses a hollow cylinder that rotates or slides to align supply holes.
  • Ceramic Disc Valves: Features two polished ceramic discs with matching slots. Sliding the discs past each other creates an extremely durable, airtight water seal.

For safety, a faucet spout must terminate above the flood level rim of the receiving basin, establishing an "air gap" that prevents siphoning of dirty sink water back into the potable water mains.

Code & Technical Regulations

Modern codes regulate water conservation (GPM rates) and require safety devices to prevent scalding in public spaces.

Faucet Type IPC Regulations (Chapter 6) UPC Regulations (Chapter 6)
Private Lavatory Limit Max flow rate of 2.2 GPM at 60 PSI (Table 604.4). Max flow rate of 2.2 GPM at 60 PSI (Section 608.2).
Public Hand-Washing Max flow rate of 0.5 GPM; metering faucets limited to 0.25 gal/cycle (Table 604.4). Max flow rate of 0.5 GPM; metering faucets limited to 0.25 gal/cycle (Section 608.2.1).
Scald Protection (Public) Must deliver water through a thermostatic mixing valve complying with ASSE 1070, max temp 110°F (Section 607.1.2). Public hot water faucets must be limited to 120°F max, and bidets to 110°F max by ASSE 1070 valves (Section 603.5).

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Water safety and conservation are highly tested items on plumbing exams:

  • Air Gap Sizing: You will be tested on the minimum required air gap. The standard is 1 inch for a 1/2-inch inlet pipe (or 1.5 inches if close to a sidewall). Memorize these dimensions in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • ASSE Valve Standards: Know the difference between ASSE 1016 (shower/tub anti-scald valves) and ASSE 1070 (water temperature limiting devices for sink faucets). Study these certifications in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Public vs. Private Sizing: Verify if the test question defines a location as "public" or "private," as this changes the allowable GPM limits. Practice these classification scenarios in General Free Prep or Tradesman & Inspector Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 607 & 608: Water temperature safety controls and backflow prevention air gaps.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 608.2: Outlines water flow limits and conservation guidelines.
  • ASME A112.18.1: Technical manufacturing standards for plumbing supply fittings.
