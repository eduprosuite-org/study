What is a Building Sewer?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A building sewer (or house sewer) is the exterior drainage pipe that extends from the end of the building drain to a public sewer system, private sewer lateral, or individual septic disposal system. It carries all of a structure's wastewater away for municipal treatment or filtration.

Core Concept & Mechanism

The building sewer serves as the final link between a building's internal plumbing network and the local municipal waste system. It starts directly where the building drain ends—defined as 30 inches outside the foundation under the IPC, or 2 feet outside under the UPC. Because the building sewer runs underground through exterior soil, it is designed to withstand local ground movements, frost loads, and potential root intrusion.

Unlike interior drain lines, which use DWV-rated Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, building sewers are often installed using SDR-35 (Standard Dimension Ratio) PVC pipe, clay pipe, or ductile iron. The pipe is sized based on the total drainage load of the structure. Cleanouts must be installed at the transition point, at every 100 feet of run, and at any change of direction exceeding 45 degrees.

A key design consideration is the water-sewer line separation. To prevent contamination of drinking water in the event of a simultaneous break in both pipes, codes require strict vertical and horizontal separation distances when routing water service lines and building sewers in the same trench.

Code & Technical Regulations

The physical dimensions, sizing rules, and line separation criteria are defined differently in the IPC and UPC codes:

Requirement Type IPC Section / Standard UPC Section / Standard
Water Line Separation Min 5 feet horizontal separation unless sewer is at least 12 inches below water line or made of approved water pipe material (Section 603.2). Min 10 feet horizontal separation. If in same trench, water line must be on solid shelf at least 12 inches above sewer (Section 720.0).
Sizing: 4-inch @ 1/4" Slope Max 180 DFUs (Table 710.1(1)). Max 250 DFUs (Table 717.1).
Approved Materials ASTM D2665, ASTM D3034, Cast Iron, Copper (Table 702.3). ASTM D2665, ASTM D3034, ABS, Cast Iron (Table 701.2).

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Candidates should pay close attention to building sewer rules, which are heavily represented in sizing sections:

  • Separation Distances: This is a classic exam question. Know the horizontal (5 ft vs. 10 ft) and vertical (12 inches) clearance rules between potable water lines and sewer pipes. Study this in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Transition Points: Be sure to distinguish between a building drain (interior and immediately exterior) and a building sewer (exterior run to city main). Note the boundary distances. Prep for these boundary scenarios in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • Cleanout Frequency: Cleanouts are required for building sewers at changes of direction, every 100 feet, and near the property line in some local jurisdictions. Review cleanout code details in the General Free Prep or the Tradesman & Inspector Exam Prep.

Official Citations

  • ICC IPC Section 202 & Section 720: Defines sewer lines and water supply separation requirements.
  • IAPMO UPC Section 204.0 & Section 717.0: Sewer definition, sizing rules, and installation standards.
  • ASTM D3034: Specification for type PSM poly-vinyl chloride (PVC) sewer pipes.
