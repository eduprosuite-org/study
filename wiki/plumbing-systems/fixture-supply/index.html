What is a Fixture Supply?

Curator: John Masterson
Published: 2026-08-16
Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A fixture supply is the final water supply pipe or flexible tube connecting an individual plumbing fixture (such as a faucet, toilet fill valve, or ice maker) to the localized wall shutoff valve.

Core Concept & Mechanism

The fixture supply represents the terminal connection of the pressurized potable water system. Cold or hot water is conveyed through building wall cavities via distribution lines. A stub-out emerges from the wall and terminates at an angle or straight stop valve. The pipe or tubing running from this stop valve to the inlet of the fixture faucet is the fixture supply.

Fixture supplies are commonly installed using one of four materials:

  • Braided Stainless Steel: Extremely popular due to its high flexibility, burst resistance, and ease of installation in tight vanity spaces.
  • Soft Copper Tubing: Rigid yet malleable, chrome-plated copper is used in high-end or commercial installations where a polished finish is required.
  • PEX Tubing: Semi-flexible plastic connection lines.
  • Solid Brass Pipe: Threaded pipe used in heavy-duty commercial or public installations.

Sizing is critical. If a supply tube is undersized, water velocity increases (leading to noisy pipes and pinhole erosion) and flow rates drop.

Code & Technical Regulations

Plumbing codes establish the absolute minimum diameters allowed for individual fixture supply tubes to maintain balanced flow rates.

Plumbing Fixture IPC Minimum Tube Diameter (Table 604.5) UPC Minimum Tube Diameter (Table 610.4)
Water Closet (Flush Tank) 3/8 inch (minimum size) 3/8 inch (minimum size)
Lavatory Sink 3/8 inch (minimum size) 3/8 inch (minimum size)
Bathtub (Standard) 1/2 inch (minimum size) 1/2 inch (minimum size)

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Pressurized supply connections are standard testing concepts on licensing exams:

  • Minimum Sizing Limits: Memorize the minimum sizes for common fixtures. While 3/8-inch is standard for toilets and sinks, bathtubs and showers require a minimum 1/2-inch line. Study this sizing chart in the Journeyman Plumber Exam Prep.
  • Prohibited Concealed Locations: Flexible braided stainless steel supply lines must be exposed and accessible. They are strictly prohibited inside walls, floors, or behind drywall. Review these safety rules in the Master Contractor Exam Prep.
  • WSFU Values: Know how to size supply manifolds by adding the WSFU values of the connected fixture branches. Prepare for these calculation questions in General Free Prep or Tradesman & Inspector Prep.

Official Citations

  • IAPMO UPC Table 610.4: Sizing chart for water fixture supply lines.
