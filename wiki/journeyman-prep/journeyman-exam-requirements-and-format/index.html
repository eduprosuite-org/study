What are the Journeyman Plumber Exam Requirements & Format?

Curator: John Masterson (Master Plumber)
Updated: August 16, 2026
Verified & Fact Checked
AI Overview & Quick Facts
A Journeyman Plumber license represents a credential certifying that a tradesperson has completed the mandatory apprentice hours and passed a rigorous state licensing examination. The exam tests plumbing science, local code compliance, and safety standards.

Core Concept & Mechanism

To earn a Journeyman Plumber license, candidates must meet strict regulatory pre-requisites. Most state licensing boards require the completion of a registered 4-to-5-year apprenticeship program, consisting of at least 8,000 hours of documented on-the-job training (OJT) and 576 hours of related classroom instruction.

Once approved by the state board, candidates register for the licensing exam, which is typically administered by third-party testing services such as PSI, Pearson VUE, or Prometric. The exam format generally consists of 80 to 120 multiple-choice questions with a time limit of 3 to 4 hours. It is an open-book format referencing the state's adopted plumbing code (either the IPC or UPC). Passing the exam requires a minimum score of 70% to 75% depending on the jurisdiction.

Code & Technical Regulations

Licensing board requirements vary by state, with some jurisdictions imposing hands-on shop assembly tests alongside the standard codebook exam.

State Jurisdiction Required OJT Hours Exam Format & Scope
Texas (TSBPE) 8,000 hours under direct supervision of a Master Plumber. Written code test (UPC) + hands-on shop test (designing and constructing lead joints and copper manifolds).
Virginia (DPOR) 4 years OJT + 240 hours of formal vocational training. Written only: 85 multiple-choice questions focusing on the Virginia Construction Code (IPC-based).
Maryland (WSSC) 4 years as a registered apprentice with 7,500 hours OJT. Written exam based on WSSC plumbing code and standard IPC layout rules.

Journeyman & Master Exam Prep Insights

Proper preparation is key to navigating the administrative and testing hurdles:

  • Document Your Hours: Maintain notarized employment records to avoid administrative delays when submitting your state board application. Access study guides at the Journeyman Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Hands-On Shop Practice: If testing in Texas or similar states, practice drawing isometric DWV schematics and soldering joints under time limits. Learn isometric drawing rules under the Master Contractor Plumbing Exam Prep.
  • Code Distribution: Master water sizing, venting limits, gas piping, and fixture clearances. Take mock exams inside the General & Free Prep Portal.
  • State Board Variations: Learn the local administrative amendments for your testing jurisdiction. Study state contractor laws at the Tradesman & Inspector Exams.
