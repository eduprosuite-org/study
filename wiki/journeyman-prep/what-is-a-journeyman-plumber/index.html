What is a Journeyman Plumber? Role, Scope & Career Hierarchy

Published: August 18, 2026 • Author: John Masterson • 8 min read

A Journeyman Plumber is a licensed trade professional who has successfully completed an accredited apprenticeship program and passed state or municipal plumbing board examinations. Unlike apprentices who must work under direct oversight, a journeyman is legally authorized to execute residential, commercial, and industrial plumbing installations and repairs completely independently.

The 3-Tier Plumbing Trade Hierarchy

The plumbing industry operates on a traditional, structured guild system designed to protect public health, sanitation, and safety. Understanding where the journeyman fits in this hierarchy is critical for apprentices and career planners:

Trade Level Experience Required Supervision Needed Can Pull Permits?
1. Apprentice Plumber 0 – 4 Years (In Training) 100% Direct Supervision No
2. Journeyman Plumber 4 – 5 Years (8,000+ Hrs OJT) Independent / Unsupervised Typically No (State Dependent)
3. Master Plumber 1 – 5 Years Post-Journeyman Full Authority / Oversees Crews Yes (Contractor Permitting)

Legal Scope of Work & Responsibilities

A licensed journeyman plumber is the backbone of job site execution. Their daily scope includes:

  • DWV (Drain-Waste-Vent) Systems: Sizing, pitching, roughing in, and testing drainage stacks, building drains, and sanitary branch piping according to the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
  • Potable Water Supply: Installing copper, PEX, and CPVC distribution systems with pressure reducing valves (PRVs) and backflow prevention assemblies.
  • Fuel Gas Piping: Sizing and pressure testing natural gas and liquid propane (LP) steel and CSST lines.
  • Fixture Trim & Water Heating: Installing tankless water heaters, commercial boilers, interceptors, and high-efficiency sanitary fixtures.
  • Apprentice Mentorship: Guiding, mentoring, and monitoring registered apprentices on active construction sites.

Journeyman vs Master Plumber: What is the Difference?

While both tiers work independently, a Master Plumber holds advanced business and engineering certifications. Master plumbers typically design plumbing system blueprints, sign off on municipal building permits, carry commercial contractor liability insurance, and employ teams of journeymen and apprentices. For detailed breakdowns, see our Master vs Contractor Plumbing Guide.

How to Pass the Licensing Exam

To transition from an apprentice to a licensed journeyman, candidates must sit for an intensive state board examination consisting of 80 to 125 open-book code questions and isometric drawings. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an apprentice and a journeyman plumber?

An apprentice is a trainee who must perform plumbing work under the direct on-site supervision of a licensed plumber. A journeyman plumber has completed 4-5 years of apprenticeship, passed a state board exam, and is legally licensed to work independently without supervision.

Can a journeyman plumber pull permits and run their own business?

In most states and municipal jurisdictions, a journeyman plumber cannot independently pull building/plumbing permits or operate an independent contracting business. Pulling permits and owning a contracting firm typically requires a Master Plumber license or a Plumbing Contractor license.

What is the origin of the term 'Journeyman'?

The term originates from the French word 'journée' (meaning a day's work). Historically in trade guilds, a journeyman was a skilled craftsman qualified to be paid by the day, having finished their apprenticeship.
