How to Become a Journeyman Plumber: Complete 5-Step Guide

Published: August 18, 2026 • Author: John Masterson • 9 min read

Becoming a licensed Journeyman Plumber transforms an entry-level worker into an autonomous, highly compensated tradesperson. It requires dedication across classroom theory and thousands of field hours. Follow this comprehensive 5-step roadmap to earn your journeyman credentials.

The 5-Step Journey to Licensure

Step 1: Satisfy Basic Prerequisites

Candidates must be at least 18 years old, possess a high school diploma or GED equivalent, hold a valid driver's license, and pass basic physical and drug screenings. Basic math proficiency (algebra and geometry for pipe offsets) is essential.

Step 2: Join an Accredited Apprenticeship Program

Choose between Union programs (United Association - UA JATC) or Open-Shop/Non-Union programs (PHCC, ABC). Register your apprentice card with your state's plumbing licensing board (e.g., TSBPE in Texas, DPOR in Virginia, or Massachusetts Division of Occupational Licensure).

Step 3: Complete 8,000 On-The-Job Hours & 576 Classroom Hours

Over 4 to 5 years, log a minimum of 8,000 documented hours under master plumber supervision. Attend weekly night classes covering plumbing codes (IPC/UPC), trade math, blueprint schematics, fuel gas piping, and OSHA safety standards.

Step 4: Submit State Exam Application & Work Verification

Complete your state examination application, submit employer affidavits verifying 8,000 hours, pay registration fees ($50–$150), and receive your test authorization from testing providers such as PSI, Prov, or Pearson VUE.

Step 5: Pass the State Journeyman Plumber Examination

Score 70% or higher on a timed open-book exam testing Drainage Fixture Units (DFU), water pipe sizing, venting limits, gas pipe volume, isometric drafting, and local administrative codes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hours of apprenticeship are required to become a journeyman plumber?

Most state licensing boards require 8,000 hours of documented on-the-job training (approx. 4–5 years) under a licensed master plumber, along with 500–600 hours of classroom instruction.

Is the Journeyman Plumber exam open book?

Yes, in most US states, the exam is open book. Test-takers bring officially tabbed copies of the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
