Plumbing Aptitude Study Guide & Formulas

Passing the apprentice aptitude exam requires mastering specific arithmetic and mechanical formulas. Use this reference sheet to review key concepts before taking our practice test.

1. Trade Mathematics & Pipe Calculations

Plumbers work in precise increments of feet and inches. You must be able to add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions without a calculator.

Common Formulas:

Pipe Slope & Fall: Total Fall = Horizontal Run (feet) × Slope (inches per foot)

Example: If a drain line runs 16 feet horizontally at a 1/4 inch per foot slope, the total fall required is: 16 × 1/4 = 4 inches.

Fraction Conversions:

Convert measurements by finding a common denominator. In trade work, denominators are typically powers of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16).
Example: 3/4 + 5/8 = 6/8 + 5/8 = 11/8 = 1-3/8 inches.

2. Mechanical Reasoning & Physics Laws

Simple Lever Systems (Class-1): Load × Distance from Fulcrum = Effort × Distance from Fulcrum
Gear Speed & Teeth: RPM_Driver × Teeth_Driver = RPM_Driven × Teeth_Driven

Remember: Mating gears turn in opposite directions. Idle gears (between driver and driven) change rotation direction but do not impact the gear speed ratio.

Pulley Mechanical Advantage (MA):

Counting the number of rope segments supporting a movable load gives the Mechanical Advantage. The force required is equal to the total weight divided by the MA.

3. Spatial Relations & Isometric Drawings

Plumbing blue-prints rely on 3D isometric projections and orthogonal (top-down, front-face) views. Practice imagining the piping shape as you pivot it in 3D space.

Pivoting Rules:

  • Orthographic Projection: Translating a 3D line routing into a 2D top-down view (plan view). Vertical pipes show up as a simple circular dot, while horizontal runs are lines.
  • Block Stacking: Calculating total volume by counting hidden cubes. Check the supporting structure below high blocks to estimate the number of cubes you cannot see.
