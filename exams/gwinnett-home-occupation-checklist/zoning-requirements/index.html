Gwinnett County Home Occupation Standards

1. Verify Jurisdiction and Zoning Rules

Before applying for a business license, determine if your property is in unincorporated Gwinnett County or within a specific municipality (such as Lawrenceville, Duluth, or Norcross). Municipalities issue their own business licenses (Occupational Tax Certificates) and have local zoning guidelines.

If you reside in unincorporated Gwinnett, your business must comply with Gwinnett County's **Unified Development Ordinance (UDO)**. Home occupations are permitted in all residential zoning districts as an accessory use, provided they fulfill strict criteria to preserve neighborhood character.

2. Home Occupation Standards (UDO Rules)

Gwinnett County prohibits home-based businesses from disrupting the residential feel of neighborhoods. Review these strict guidelines:

  • Space Limitation: No more than 25% of the total gross floor area of your home may be dedicated to business activities.
  • No Non-Resident Employees: Only residents living in the home may work in the home-based business. No outside employees are permitted to work on-site.
  • Signage and Exterior Appearance: There must be no visible evidence of the business from the street (no signs, display banners, or outdoor storage of inventory).
  • Client Visits & Sales: You cannot run a retail store where clients visit to purchase products directly. On-premises assembly or group instruction is restricted.

3. Required Georgia Tax Affidavits

To register your business, Gwinnett County requires specific legal affidavits regarding your legal status and hiring practices:

U.S. Citizenship/Alien Affidavit: You must provide a notarized affidavit verifying your legal status in the United States along with a secure, verifiable photo ID (such as a driver's license or passport).

E-Verify Affidavit: If you have more than 10 employees, you must provide your E-Verify identification number. If you have fewer than 10, you must submit a signed exemption form.

4. Online Registration Portal (Citizen Access)

Submit your application digitally via the Gwinnett County Citizen Access Portal. You will need your NAICS code (Business classification code), projected gross revenue (as taxes are calculated based on gross receipts), and local address verification.
