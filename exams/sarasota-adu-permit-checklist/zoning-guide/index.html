Sarasota County ADU Zoning Guide

1. Verify Zoning and Parcel Feasibility

Before designing your ADU, verify if Accessory Dwelling Units are allowed on your parcel. Under the Sarasota County Zoning Ordinance, ADUs are permitted as accessory uses in most residential districts (including RSF-1, RSF-2, RSF-3, and RSF-4) subject to specific criteria.

Locate your property classification via the Sarasota Central Appraisal District or the county interactive GIS map. Verify there are no deed restrictions or Homeowner Association (HOA) covenants that explicitly prohibit secondary units, as local zoning laws cannot supersede private HOA bylaws.

2. Setback, Size, and Height Limits

To qualify for an administrative permit approval without a variance hearing, your ADU must satisfy the following development criteria:

  • Maximum Floor Area: The living space cannot exceed 750 square feet (or 800 square feet under specific urban growth overlays) or 40% of the principal structure's living area, whichever is smaller.
  • Setbacks: The secondary structure must adhere to the same side and front setbacks as the primary house. The rear setback is typically a minimum of 10 feet.
  • Height: Detached ADUs cannot exceed 20 feet in height, or the height of the primary dwelling, whichever is lower.

3. Utility Connections & Impact Fees

Sarasota County regulates how utilities are distributed to accessory structures. The ADU can be linked to the primary structure's water meter, electric panel, and sewer lateral.

If your property uses an **On-Site Sewage Disposal System (Septic)**, the Florida Department of Health must inspect and approve the system to ensure it has the capacity for the additional bedrooms/bathrooms. A separate septic tank may be required if the primary system is undersized.

4. Plan Review and Submission

Applications must be submitted electronically through the Sarasota County Land Information Management System (LIMS). Your submission must include a sealed professional site plan, structural engineering drawings conforming to the **Florida Building Code (Wind load design)**, and energy calculations.
