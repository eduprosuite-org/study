Complete DRE License Exam Math Study Guide

The California Department of Real Estate (DRE) licensing exams contain a significant portion of mathematical questions designed to test your competence in transaction accounting, property valuation, and land calculations. Mastering these formulas is essential to secure your license on your first try.

1. Commission Splits and Fee Calculations

Commissions are typically calculated as a percentage of the total property sales price. On the exam, you will often face complex "split" scenarios, where the total commission is shared between the listing and selling brokers, and then split again with their respective salespeople.

Commission = Sales Price × Commission Rate (%)
Listing/Selling Split = Total Commission × Split Percentage (%)
Salesperson Share = Broker Split × Agent Split (%)

Exam Tip: Read carefully to identify whether the question is asking for the total commission, the broker's split, or the salesperson's final share. Missing one step is the most common source of error.

2. Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate) & Net Operating Income (NOI)

Capitalization Rate measures the rate of return on an investment property based on its income. To solve valuation problems using Cap Rate, you must understand the IRV formula (Income, Rate, Value).

Net Operating Income (NOI) = Gross Scheduled Income - Vacancy/Loss - Operating Expenses
Capitalization Rate = NOI ÷ Property Value (Price)
Estimated Value = NOI ÷ Capitalization Rate

Critical Trick: Under DRE rules, **operating expenses** exclude mortgage interest, principal payments (debt service), and income tax. If the question mentions debt service, do NOT subtract it from Gross Income to find NOI.

3. Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)

GRM is a quick index used to estimate the value of residential rental properties (usually duplexes or single-family rental homes). It does not take expenses into account, only gross rent.

Monthly GRM = Purchase Price ÷ Gross Monthly Rent
Estimated Value = Gross Monthly Rent × Monthly GRM

Important Distinction: Ensure you are using **monthly** rent with a monthly multiplier, or **annual** rent with an annual multiplier. If the question gives monthly rent and asks for an annual GRM, multiply the monthly rent by 12 first.

4. Loan-to-Value (LTV) and Mortgage Calculations

LTV is the relationship between the loan amount and the purchase price or appraised value of the property (whichever is lower). Lenders use LTV to define risk limits.

LTV Ratio = Loan Amount ÷ Property Value
Loan Amount = Property Value × LTV Ratio
Down Payment = Property Value - Loan Amount

5. Land Measurement & Cost per Square Foot

DRE questions frequently test your ability to convert square footage into acreage. The magic conversion number you must memorize is **43,560**.

Total Square Feet = Width (Frontage) × Depth
Acreage = Total Square Feet ÷ 43,560 sq ft
Cost per Sq Ft = Purchase Price ÷ Total Square Feet

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How many math questions are on the California Real Estate Exam?

Typically, math-related questions make up about 10% to 15% of the total exam (approx. 15 out of 150 questions for salespersons). These include commissions, LTV, taxes, and valuations.

Are calculators allowed in the California Real Estate Exam?

Yes, basic, non-programmable calculators are provided by the testing center (or allowed to be brought in based on standard DRE guidelines). Our simulator includes a built-in virtual equivalent.

How is Net Operating Income (NOI) calculated on the real estate exam?

Net Operating Income (NOI) is calculated by taking the Gross Scheduled Income, subtracting vacancy and collection losses to get Effective Gross Income (EGI), and then subtracting operating expenses (excluding mortgage interest and principal payments).

How many square feet are in an acre?

There are exactly **43,560** square feet in one acre. A helpful mnemonic device to remember this is: "4 old ladies driving down Interstate 80 at 55 miles per hour" (4, 3, 5, 6, 0).
